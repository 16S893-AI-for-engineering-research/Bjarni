project outline

From one fuel, one shape
to thirteen fuels, four shapes.

TASOPT (Transport Aircraft System OPTimization) is a physics-based aircraft sizing & optimization tool. Today it's very good at one thing: conventional tube-and-wing airplanes burning conventional, Jet-A-like fuel. This project uses an AI coding agent to rapidly extend TASOPT's modelling capability along two axes at once — fuel and airframe — so we can quantify how radically different fuels reshape the aircraft that has to carry them.

the constraint today

TASOPT's current box

Tube-and-wing fuselages only. Fuels that behave like Jet-A — similar density, similar energy content, stored as an ambient liquid, dropped straight into a wing tank. That's a fine assumption for kerosene. It falls apart the moment you try hydrogen, ammonia, or methanol, where energy density, storage temperature, and tank volume all move by factors of 2–14×.

axis 1 — fuel

Thirteen fuels, one reference

Jet-A stays in as the reference case. Alongside it: three families of alternative fuel, each breaking a different TASOPT assumption.

Drop-in SAF family

HEFA, Fischer–Tropsch, Alcohol-to-Jet, e-Kerosene (PtL). Chemically close to Jet-A — the "easy" cases that stress-test the fuel-property interface without breaking tank geometry.

Cryogenic fuels

Liquid hydrogen and liquid methane. Huge gravimetric energy density, terrible volumetric density (LH2 especially) — insulated cryo tanks want to eat the fuselage.

Non-drop-in liquids

Ammonia, methanol, ethanol, butanol, DME, and LOHC. Ambient-ish handling, but energy density roughly half of Jet-A — tanks get big, airframes get long, or both.

axis 2 — airframe

Four configurations, spanning regional to ultra-widebody

Different fuels reward different fuselage shapes — a cryogenic tank wants volume, not a slender tube. So we expand the airframe library alongside the fuel library, and cross the two with aircraft size class.

how we get there

Why an AI coding agent, specifically

TASOPT's Fortran/Julia codebase encodes a lot of institutional knowledge about tube-and-wing, Jet-A aircraft. Extending it to 12 new fuels and 3 new airframe families by hand is a multi-year slog of module-by-module rewrites. The bet here: use an AI agent to accelerate that re-derivation — new weight models, new tank/thermal models, new mission-fuel-burn coupling — while a human (hi) keeps the physics honest.

1. Fuel property layer

Generalize the fuel model: energy density, density vs. temperature, storage phase, boil-off — parametrized, not hard-coded to Jet-A.

2. Airframe geometry layer

Add D8, BWB, and TBW geometry & weight modules alongside the existing tube-and-wing model.

3. Coupled optimization

Let the optimizer re-balance wing loading, tank placement, and mission profile for each fuel × config × size combination.

what's next in the PhD

From pre-screening to system impact

The work so far has been high-level system screening of alternative fuels — figuring out which ones are even worth a deeper look. The next step is exactly what this tool enables: modelling the two-way coupling between fuel choice and aircraft design, to understand the total system impact of switching fuels — not just at the tank, but across the whole airplane.
